The Psychology of Post-Breakup Behavior

When a relationship ends, the "dumper" rarely walks away feeling nothing. However, human beings are incredibly complex. To protect their ego and justify their decision, an ex will often suppress their feelings of missing you. This is known as Cognitive Dissonance. The behavioral clues they exhibit are often subconscious leaks of their true emotional state.

Fading Affect Bias

Psychologists have identified a phenomenon called the "Fading Affect Bias." This means that over time, the human brain naturally forgets negative emotional events faster than positive ones. If your ex broke up with you because of specific arguments, those memories will fade. What remains are the memories of the connection you shared. This is why an ex often starts to miss you most around the 30-day mark.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do exes regret breaking up? ▼

Studies suggest that up to 68% of people who initiate a breakup experience regret at some point. However, they rarely admit it immediately. It usually happens after the initial "relief" phase wears off and the loneliness sets in.

Why does my ex view my social media but not text me? ▼

This is called "passive monitoring." They want the dopamine hit of seeing your face and knowing you are okay, but they are too stubborn or scared to actually initiate contact and risk rejection.
